# Lecturer methodology pack: layperson-to-technical talking points

## Slide 1 — the methodology in one sentence

**Simple:** every analysed episode uses the same genome-reconstruction route.

**Technical:** the analytical cohort is 532 selected QC-passing Longcycler episodes from 161 residents, with 16 operational UTI and 516 operational Not_UTI episodes.

## Slide 2 — why the numbers differ

532 counts episodes, 161 counts residents, 893 counts all direct within-resident pairs, and 371 counts adjacent transitions after each timeline is rebuilt. These are different units, not a shrinking queue.

## Slide 3 — clinical phenotype

The culture-plus-symptom rule is the current versioned operational phenotype. It is assigned before genomic analysis, Not_UTI is not a healthy control, and the 17 near-miss rows remain separate.

## Slide 4 — selected cohort flow

The selected manifest contains one QC-passing Longcycler assembly per exact episode key. Clinical and genomic keys match before downstream analysis. The full 583-episode clinical source is attrition/QC context only.

## Slide 5 — parallel genomic branches

VFDB describes detected gene features; MLST provides lineage context; direct dnadiff pairs provide pair-specific distance evidence; Parsnp core-genome and Panaroo pangenome branches provide population context. One method does not substitute for another.

## Slide 6 — denominator defence

The release contains 893 direct pairs and 371 adjacent transitions from 139 residents. 140 adjacent transitions are at or below 25 SNPs. Among 9 Not_UTI-to-UTI transitions, 5 meet that operational boundary.

## Slide 7 — thresholds and limits

The 25-SNP boundary is operational, not universal. The UTI phenotype is operational, not the full published protocol. Completeness, contamination and read coverage are not part of the implemented assembly-QC screen.

## Slide 8 — traceability

RQ01–RQ10 are the numbered release layer. The focused casebook contains 9 cases, 9 linked and 0 missing; 17 near-miss rows remain a separate audit set.
