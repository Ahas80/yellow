# Final Walkthrough: Virulence Factor (VF) Dynamics

This document provides a narrative explanation of the generated visualisations, manuscript-ready captions, and drafting text for the abstract and limitations sections based on the comprehensive VF analysis of the YELLOW RoUTIne cohort.

## Narrative Explanation of Figures

**Figure 1 (Burden)** establishes the baseline distribution of VF gene counts. While the absolute number of VF genes per isolate is generally high across all statuses (median 82 genes), there is no massive shift in total *number* of genes distinguishing ASB, UTI, or Negative episodes. The distributions overlap substantially, indicating that total quantitative "VF burden" alone does not differentiate infection from colonization.

**Figure 2 (Prevalence & Enrichment)** moves from total counts to specific genes. The lollipop plot and forest plot reveal that while few genes achieve strict statistical significance after multiple-testing correction (partly due to the small $n=16$ UTI sample size relative to $n=136$ ASB), there is a distinct signal indicating enrichment of the *iroBCDEN* (salmochelin) iron acquisition operon and the *astA* toxin in UTI episodes. This suggests qualitative differences in *which* genes are present rather than merely *how many*.

**Figure 3 (Category Profiles)** groups these specific genes by function. When aggregating genes into functional categories (Toxins, Iron Acquisition, Adhesion, etc.), we see fairly conserved profiles across all clinical states. This reinforces the finding that the core uropathogenic machinery is highly prevalent even in ASB and Negative groups, making the subtle relative enrichment of specific operons like *iro* more notable.

**Figure 4 (Longitudinal Stability)** leverages the strengths of the study design by examining within-person dynamics across 96 consecutive transitions. It reveals remarkable intra-patient stability over time: 72.9% of all consecutive timepoint transitions show absolutely zero change in VF gene profile (Jaccard similarity = 1.0). When focusing specifically on the 12 observed transitions from ASB to UTI, 5 cases involved discrete VF gene gains. Strikingly, the most commonly gained genes during this transition were exactly those identified as enriched in the cross-sectional analysis: the *iro* operon. 

**Figure 5 (Confounding Checks)** acts as a critical reality check on our data structure. It visualizes the structural confound in the dataset: all 16 UTI episodes occurred at the unscheduled "Uricult" timepoint, whereas all 136 ASB episodes occurred at scheduled timepoints (T0, T1, T2). It also shows a paired before-and-after view of patients who had both a scheduled visit and a Uricult visit. This structural separation emphasizes that all UTI vs ASB comparisons are inherently confounded by the visit type (scheduled vs symptom-triggered), which must be acknowledged explicitly in any formal reporting.

## Manuscript-Ready Figure Captions

**Figure 1. Distribution of total Virulence Factor (VF) gene burden by clinical status.** (A) Raincloud plot showing the density and distribution of total VF genes (out of 164 queried) per isolate across ASB ($n=136$), UTI ($n=16$), and Negative ($n=31$) episodes. Each point represents a single sequenced isolate. (B) Participant-aggregated boxplots showing the mean VF gene count per participant, classified by their clinical status history, controlling for pseudo-replication. (C) VF gene burden stratified by study timepoint, highlighting the distribution of scores across scheduled visits up to T2 and the event-triggered Uricult visit.

**Figure 2. VF gene prevalence differences and enrichment between UTI and ASB episodes.** (A) Lollipop plot highlighting the top 20 genes with the greatest difference in absolute prevalence (percentage points) when comparing isolates from UTI episodes ($n=16$) relative to ASB episodes ($n=136$). Genes extending to the right are more prevalent in UTI; to the left, more prevalent in ASB. (B) Forest plot of unadjusted odds ratios (log scale) from Fisher's exact tests comparing gene presence in UTI vs ASB. Error bars represent 95% confidence intervals. The *iro* operon genes and *astA* show the strongest signals of enrichment, though most do not survive stringent multiple testing correction due to sample size limitations.

**Figure 3. VF category composition across clinical statuses.** (A) Stacked bar chart showing the average percentage breakdown of VF functional categories (Adhesion/Fimbriae, Iron Acquisition, Protectins, Toxins, Unassigned) within the total VF repertoire of each clinical status group. (B) Boxplots of raw gene counts per isolate broken down by functional category and clinical status, demonstrating generally conserved functional profiles. (C) Heatmap of median gene counts per category across the three clinical statuses.

**Figure 4. Within-person longitudinal stability and dynamics of VF profiles.** (A) Histograms of Jaccard similarity scores between consecutive paired isolates from the same participant ($n=96$ transitions), stratified by the clinical status transition (e.g., ASB $\rightarrow$ ASB, ASB $\rightarrow$ UTI). A Jaccard score of 1.0 indicates identical VF profiles. (B) Slopegraph tracking absolute VF counts for individual participants between consecutive timepoints. (C) The most frequently gained individual VF genes specifically during transitions from ASB colonization to acute UTI ($n=12$ transitions, of which 5 exhibited non-zero gene gains). The salmochelin (*iro*) iron acquisition operon components were the most frequently acquired genes leading into an infection episode. (D) Heatmap highlighting specific qualitative gene changes (gains or losses) for individual participants transitioning from ASB to UTI.

**Figure 5. Assessment of visit-type confounding and within-person paired analysis.** (A) Tile plot showing the distribution of the 183 sequenced episodes across clinical status and study timepoint, illustrating that 100% of UTI episodes correspond to the symptom-triggered "Uricult" timepoint while ASB episodes occurred exclusively at scheduled visits. (B-C) Total VF burden plotted specifically for isolates exclusively from scheduled visits (B) or Uricult visits (C) to isolate the effect of timepoint. (D) Paired slopegraph restricting analysis to the 14 participants who contributed both a scheduled sequence and a Uricult sequence, showing the trajectory of their mean VF count.

## Suggested Abstract "Results" Paragraph

Total virulence factor (VF) gene burden was high across all clinical states (median 82 genes per isolate) and did not quantitatively distinguish asymptomatic bacteriuria (ASB) from symptomatic infection (UTI). However, qualitative differences emerged: UTI isolates were enriched for specific factors, most notably the salmochelin iron-acquisition operon (*iroBCDEN*) and the *astA* toxin. Longitudinally, within-patient VF profiles were remarkably stable across consecutive timepoints, with 72.9% of transitions showing identical gene repertoires. In the rare instances where patients progressed from ASB to UTI and exhibited a shift in VF profile, the *iro* operon genes were the most frequently acquired factors prior to infection, suggesting specific iron-scavenging capabilities may drive transition from colonization to acute pathogenesis.

## Suggested "Limitations" Paragraph

Our analysis is subject to important limitations regarding statistical power and study design. The sample size for clinically confirmed UTI episodes with retrievable sequence data was small ($n=16$), severely limiting the statistical power of robust inferential models or multiple testing corrections, reducing confidence in specific gene enrichments. Furthermore, there is a strict structural confound between clinical status and sample collection strategy: all UTI isolates were collected during unscheduled, symptom-triggered visits, whereas ASB isolates originated from scheduled surveillance timepoints. Consequently, we cannot formally disentangle differences driven by true pathogenic status from those driven by the inherent differences between routine screening and acute event presentation.
